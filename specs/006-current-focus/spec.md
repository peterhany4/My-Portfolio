# Feature Specification: 006 — Current Focus / Learning Section

## Overview

Present what Peter Hany is currently learning and building, highlighting his backend development learning, full-stack direction, software engineering principles (architecture, problem-solving, engineering practices), and ability to evolve as his learning progresses.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-09-10
- Q: Are there any ambiguities regarding current learning focus areas or terminology? → A: None. Content aligns with authentic representation rules and avoids misleading skill percentages or inflated claims.

## User Scenarios & Testing

### Scenario 1: Viewing Current Learning Direction and Backend Focus
- **Actor**: Site Visitor / Recruiter
- **Description**: A visitor views the Current Focus section to understand what technologies and engineering practices Peter is currently exploring.
- **Acceptance Criteria**:
  - Clear visual grouping of current learning areas (backend development, full-stack progression, software engineering practices).
  - Absence of misleading skill percentage bars (e.g., React 90%).
  - Clear progression labels (Current, Exploring, Learning, Experienced).

## Functional Requirements

### FR1: Current Learning Areas
- The Current Focus section MUST accurately represent backend development learning and full-stack direction.
- The section MUST highlight interest in software architecture, problem-solving, and engineering practices.

### FR2: Presentation Quality
- The section MUST utilize visual grouping without misleading percentage/skill bars.
- Progression MUST be communicated using honest, qualitative markers (Current, Exploring, Learning).
- Content MUST remain easy to update as learning progresses.

## Success Criteria

- **Measurable & Verifiable Outcomes**:
  - Visitors understand Peter's current learning focus and growth trajectory within 15 seconds.
  - Zero misleading proficiency metrics or skill percentages appear on the site.

## Assumptions & Constraints
- The Current Focus section sits immediately below the About section in the single-page scroll layout.
